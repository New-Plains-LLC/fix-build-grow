A flickering light is annoying, but it's also your home trying to tell you something. Sometimes the fix is as simple as tightening a bulb. Other times it's a sign of a loose wire connection that needs attention before it becomes a fire hazard.

This guide walks you through every possible cause — from the easiest fix to the most serious — so you can diagnose and solve the problem safely.

{{< warning >}}
If your lights flicker AND you notice burning smells, buzzing sounds, or warm switch plates, turn off the breaker immediately and call a licensed electrician. These are signs of arcing or overheating, not a simple loose bulb.
{{< /warning >}}

## Step 1: Check the Bulb (The Most Common Fix)

Nine times out of ten, a flickering light is caused by a loose bulb. This is especially common in ceiling fans, recessed cans, and garage fixtures where vibration works bulbs loose over time.

**What to do:**
- Turn off the light switch and let the bulb cool completely.
- Screw the bulb tighter by hand — do not overtighten.
- If the bulb is a fluorescent or CFL, try twisting it until it fully seats in the socket.
- If the flickering stops, you're done.

**If it didn't work:** Remove the bulb and inspect the bottom contact tab in the socket. If it's flattened or pushed down, use a non-conductive tool (plastic toothpick or wooden skewer) to gently pry it up slightly so it makes better contact with the bulb tip.

{{< callout >}}
**Pro tip:** LED bulbs can flicker even when new. If you recently switched from incandescent to LED, your dimmer switch may not be compatible. More on that in Step 4.
{{< /callout >}}

## Step 2: Try a Different Bulb

A failing bulb can flicker before it burns out completely. Swap the flickering bulb with a known-good bulb from another fixture in the same room.

- If the flickering follows the suspect bulb to the new fixture, replace the bulb — it's dying.
- If the flickering stays in the original fixture with a different bulb, the problem is in the fixture or wiring.

## Step 3: Tighten the Bulb in the Socket — and Check the Socket

If a standard bulb still fits loosely after tightening, the socket's inner contacts may be worn or spread apart.

**To check and tighten socket contacts:**

1. Turn off the breaker to that light fixture — do not rely on the wall switch alone.
2. Remove the bulb.
3. Look inside the socket at the brass tab at the bottom center.
4. If it looks flattened, gently pry it up about ⅛ inch using a wooden skewer or plastic pick — never use a metal screwdriver for this.
5. Reinstall the bulb and restore power.

{{< warning >}}
Never stick your fingers or metal tools into a live socket. Always kill power at the breaker before working inside any light fixture.
{{< /warning >}}

## Step 4: Check the Dimmer Switch Compatibility

If your light is on a dimmer and uses LED or CFL bulbs, the dimmer may not be rated for those bulb types.

**Quick compatibility test:**
- Turn the dimmer to 100% brightness. If the flickering stops, it's a compatibility issue.
- Check that your dimmer switch is labeled "LED-compatible" or "CL-rated" (CFL/LED).
- Check that your bulb packaging says "dimmable."

**Fix:** Replace the dimmer with an LED-compatible model ($15–$30) or switch to dimmable LED bulbs. Both approaches solve about 80% of dimmer-related flickering.

## Step 5: Tighten Wire Connections in the Switch or Fixture

If steps 1–4 didn't solve it, the cause is likely a loose wire connection behind the switch or inside the fixture canopy. This is the most serious DIY-level cause.

**What you'll need:** Voltage tester, screwdriver (flathead and Phillips), wire nut tool or pliers.

**How to check the wall switch:**

1. Turn off the breaker to the room.
2. Remove the switch plate cover screws.
3. Unscrew the switch from the electrical box and pull it out gently — do not touch any bare wires yet.
4. Use a non-contact voltage tester to confirm no power is present.
5. Check that all wire nut connections are tight — twist each nut clockwise to snug it.
6. Check that the wires under the screw terminals on the switch are snug — about ⅛ inch of bare copper should be under each screw head.
7. Reassemble and test.

{{< callout >}}
**Same check applies to the light fixture itself.** Remove the fixture canopy (the decorative cover against the ceiling or wall) and verify the wire connections inside are tight. Loose connections inside the fixture box are a common source of flickering in pendant lights and chandeliers.
{{< /callout >}}

{{< warning >}}
Loose wire connections generate heat and can cause arcing — a leading cause of electrical fires. If you find scorch marks, melted wire insulation, or a burnt smell on any wire, STOP and call an electrician.
{{< /warning >}}

## Step 6: Check for Voltage Fluctuations (The Whole-House Test)

If multiple lights across different rooms flicker at the same time — especially when a large appliance like an AC unit, refrigerator, or washing machine kicks on — you may have a voltage fluctuation issue rather than a fixture problem.

**What to look for:**
- Lights dim briefly when the AC compressor starts — this is normal in many homes.
- Lights flicker continuously or frequently — this could mean an undersized electrical panel, loose service connection, or utility supply problem.

**What to do:**
- Tighten the main breaker connections (inside the panel, at the meter) — only do this if you're comfortable working inside a live panel. If not, call an electrician.
- Contact your utility company — they can check for loose connections on their side of the meter for free.

## When to Call a Professional

Some flickering causes are NOT DIY territory. Call a licensed electrician if:

- Flickering affects multiple circuits throughout the house
- You find scorch marks, melted insulation, or burnt odors near switches or fixtures
- The flickering is accompanied by buzzing from the fixture or switch
- You've checked everything above and the problem persists
- The breaker trips repeatedly when the light is on

{{< callout >}}
**Bottom line:** A single flickering fixture is almost always a simple fix — tighten the bulb, swap it, or check the dimmer. Whole-house flickering is a different animal and usually needs a pro. Start with the easy stuff, escalate when you have to.
{{< /callout >}}

## Summary Checklist

| Cause | Fix | Cost | Difficulty |
|-------|-----|------|------------|
| Loose bulb | Tighten by hand | Free | Easiest |
| Failing bulb | Replace bulb | $2–$10 | Easy |
| Worn socket contact | Pry up brass tab | Free | Easy |
| Incompatible dimmer | Replace dimmer or bulbs | $15–$30 | Moderate |
