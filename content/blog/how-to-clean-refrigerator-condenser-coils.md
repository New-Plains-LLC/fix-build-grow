Your refrigerator's condenser coils are the part that dumps heat out of the fridge and into your kitchen. When they're coated in dust and pet hair, that heat has nowhere to go. The compressor runs longer, works hotter, and wears out years early — and a compressor replacement usually costs more than the fridge is worth.

Cleaning the coils is a 30-minute job that needs no tools most of the time. If your fridge runs constantly, the sides feel hot, or the kitchen feels warmer than it should, this is the first thing to check.

## Why Dirty Coils Matter

The coils are a radiator. Dust acts like a blanket on that radiator. The compressor has to run longer to hit the same temperature, which shows up three ways:

- **Higher electric bill** — a fridge with fouled coils can use noticeably more power
- **Warm food or soft ice cream** — the fridge can't keep up during heavy use
- **Shortened compressor life** — heat is what kills compressors, and heat is exactly what trapped dust causes

Most manufacturers recommend cleaning the coils every 6 to 12 months. If you have pets that shed, cut that to every 3 to 6 months.

## Find Your Coils First

Coils live in one of two places, depending on your fridge:

1. **Underneath, behind the toe kick** — most modern fridges. Remove the thin grille at the bottom front; the coils are the black tube grid you see behind it.
2. **On the back** — older or budget models. Pull the fridge away from the wall and the coils are the grid mounted on the back panel.

{{< callout >}}Check your model's manual (search the model number online) before pulling anything apart. Some fridges have a fan behind the toe kick that should not be blocked while running.{{< /callout >}}

## What You Need

- Coil cleaning brush (a long, flexible brush made for this — about $8) or a soft-bristle brush on an extension
- Vacuum with a hose and brush attachment
- Flashlight
- Optional: microfiber cloth, spray bottle of water

## Step-by-Step: Cleaning Under-Fridge Coils

1. **Unplug the fridge** or shut off its breaker. You'll be working near a fan and electrical parts — no exceptions.
2. **Pull the fridge forward** enough to work behind the toe kick. Front-line it on the feet, don't drag it by the doors.
3. **Remove the toe kick grille** — most snap off by hand or are held by two screws.
4. **Vacuum first.** Use the hose attachment to pull out the loose dust, hair, and dust bunnies from around the coils, fan, and compressor. Get the big stuff out before it becomes airborne.
5. **Brush the coils.** Slide the coil brush between the tubes and work it back and forth. Brush, then vacuum what falls. Repeat across the whole grid. Don't bend the tubes — they're soft copper and a kink means a service call.
6. **Wipe down the fan blades and compressor area** with a barely damp cloth if they're grimy.
7. **Reattach the grille, push the fridge back, and plug it in.** Leave a small gap (about an inch) between the fridge and the wall so air can circulate.

For back-mounted coils, the same process applies — vacuum first, brush gently, vacuum again — but you don't need to remove anything.

## Warnings and Common Mistakes

{{< warning >}}Always unplug the fridge before cleaning. The condenser fan can start unexpectedly on some models, and the compressor housing can be hot.{{< /warning >}}

- **Don't use water or cleaners on the coils themselves.** Moisture plus electrical components plus a fridge that's plugged back in is a bad combination. Vacuum and brush do all the work.
- **Don't bend the coil tubes.** They're thin and soft. Gentle back-and-forth brush strokes only.
- **Don't use a shop vac at full power directly against the coils.** You can dent fins and tubes. Medium suction, brush attachment, some distance.
- **Don't shove the fridge fully back against the wall.** Coils need airflow. No gap means you'll be back here sooner.

## How to Tell It Worked

- The fridge cycles normally instead of running nonstop
- The toe kick area or back panel is no longer hot to the touch (warm is fine, hot is not)
- Fresh food and freezer temps hold steady — 37°F and 0°F respectively, if you have a thermometer

If the fridge still runs constantly and the coils are clean, check the door gasket (a torn seal makes the compressor run nonstop) and make sure the interior vents aren't blocked by food.

## When to Call a Pro

Call an appliance repair tech if:

- Coils are clean but the fridge still runs constantly or can't hold temperature
- You hear the compressor clicking on and off every few minutes (a sign of a failing start relay or compressor)
- You find a kinked, crushed, or oily-looking coil — refrigerant issues are not DIY

A coil cleaning costs nothing and takes 30 minutes. A compressor replacement costs $450 to $1,200 and is the most common reason a fridge gets scrapped. This is one of the highest-value half-hours in home maintenance.
